Eviction in Bangladesh: the lawful walkthrough

PRCA 1991 grounds, the 30-day notice, and why "self-help" eviction can cost you more than the rent.

📅 Jul 14, 2026⏱ 7 min read🏷 Legal · PRCA 1991

Eviction is the most emotionally charged part of property management — and the most legally dangerous for owners who improvise. In Bangladesh, the Premises Rent Control Act 1991 (PRCA 1991) governs who can be evicted, when, and how.

The lawful grounds

  • Arrears of rent — the tenant is liable for two months' rent or more, and fails to pay within one month of a written demand.
  • Subletting without consent — the premises were assigned or sublet against the agreement.
  • Breach of contract — a material term of the tenancy is broken.
  • Bona fide requirement — the owner (or family) genuinely needs the premises for their own residence or business.
  • Alteration or damage — the premises are changed or used in a way that reduces their value.

The 30-day notice trap

For most grounds, the landlord must first serve a notice to quit — typically 30 days in writing, delivered so it can be proven. A verbal warning, a WhatsApp message, or a call from the caretaker does not count. The notice period must be complete before any suit is filed; filing early gets the case thrown out on a technicality.

Self-help is illegal: changing locks, cutting utilities, or physically removing a tenant without a court order is a criminal act in itself — and it gives the tenant grounds to sue you. Even after the notice expires, only a Rent Controller's order authorizes eviction.

The realistic timeline

  1. Written demand/notice served and documented — Day 0.
  2. Notice period runs (30 days, or per contract).
  3. Petition before the Rent Controller with the tenancy agreement, rent receipts and the notice.
  4. Hearing and decree — typically 4–9 months depending on the bench.
  5. Execution by court process; the tenant is removed by the bailiff, not by you.
